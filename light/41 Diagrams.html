Rules & guidelines

How diagrams are made.

Diagrams are treated as first-class illustration assets. They share the same colour tokens, typography and geometric primitives as the rest of the Intelligaia visual system.

Purpose

Explain in one glance.

A diagram earns its place when it makes a process, structure or relationship legible in under ten seconds. If it takes longer, cut it or split it.

Colour

Brand palette only.

Yellow, ink, purple as brand-hero. Secondary tints for grouping. No off-palette colours. Same 30-token colour system as the illustration library.

Typography

Oswald + Geist.

Oswald Medium for headings, Geist Regular for labels and body. Same seven-step scale — no other weights, no other families.

Geometry

Circle · Rectangle · Curved rectangle.

Every glyph and container is built from the three primitives that govern the illustration system. Icons live inside circles; connectors are curved rectangles; blocks are rectangles.

Grid

Aligned to the 50 × 50 unit grid.

Every node, arrow and label snaps to the shared grid. Toggle the golden-rule grid on catalog pages to see the underlying construction.

Flow

Left-to-right, top-to-bottom.

Follow the reader's natural scan path. Arrows and connectors read cause → effect. No crossing lines. No hidden dependencies.

Density

Fewer, larger nodes.

Prefer 5–9 nodes over 15+. If a diagram needs more, split it into a sequence. Density kills glance-legibility.

Labelling

Short verbs and nouns.

2–4 words per label. Sentence case. No jargon unless the audience owns it. Every label sits inside or immediately beside its node — never dangling.

Purpose tag

Every diagram declares its use.

Social post · Internal explainer · Site map · Deck · Journey map. The tag drives crop, aspect ratio and export format.
