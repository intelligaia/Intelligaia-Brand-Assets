01 · Starting condition

A pass or fail hinged on a few centimetres of rubber, judged by eye.

The box test is where a driving licence is won or lost. A candidate manoeuvres through a marked L-shaped box, and the decisive fault is a single tyre touching or crossing a painted line. Today an examiner calls it from the side of the box - honest work under pressure, but it carries problems no amount of diligence removes.

Subjective by design. A few centimetres, viewed at an angle, comes down to one person's eye.
Inconsistent. Standards drift between examiners, centres and days.
Disputable. When a result is contested, there is nothing to review.
Hard to scale. Every test consumes a trained examiner in real time.
02 · How we framed it

We reframed a judgment call as a measurement.

Rather than ask a person to judge a contact of a few centimetres, we set out to measure it - against the actual painted line, in real-world units, the same way every time. The examiner's opinion becomes a number the authority can defend on appeal, and the standard is identical for every candidate.

03 · The system we built

A camera that watches the box and certifies the result.

Yard cameras stream the test live. Computer vision locks onto the candidate vehicle and each tyre, maps them onto the calibrated test box, and scores the run against the official rules - the manoeuvre sequence, boundary touches, and the three-minute limit - producing an automatic pass or fail with no manual intervention.

04 · Design and engineering together

Built in parallel - the examiner's screen and the vision pipeline.

Design and engineering ran together. On one track: a live monitoring dashboard for examiners and a public results display for the test centre. On the other: two cameras fused into one metric frame, the ground plane calibrated, and the exact tyre-ground contact pinned with segmentation. The two tracks met at a single, evidenced verdict.

05 · How it holds up

Real conditions on the test yard stress-tested it.

The constraint bit on-site. Each challenge drove a concrete fix rather than a workaround - which is what makes the result defensible.

A high, angled camera put a naive estimate on the bumper, not the tyre.
Fix: segmentation of the true tyre-ground contact.
A single view missed both-side line touches.
Fix: two cameras fused into one metric frame.
Stream latency made a real-time timer imprecise.
Fix: manual start plus an authoritative post-run analysis of the full recording.
06 · What it enabled

An objective, evidenced, repeatable licence test.

The examiner's judgment call becomes a measurement, backed by an annotated recording and a photo of every violation. Two on-site driver sessions detected line touches accurately in both - and the platform is built to extend from the box test to the full examination.

Objective. The same rule, applied the same way, every time.
Defensible. Every decision backed by timestamped photographic evidence.
Transparent. A live dashboard for examiners and a public results screen.
Extensible. A foundation ready for the full driving examination.
