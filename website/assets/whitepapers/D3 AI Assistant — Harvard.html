Whitepaper — D³ AI Assistant

Designing a research-first AI assistant for Harvard's D³ Institute.

End-to-end UX strategy, intensive user research, and AI design principles for a platform that transforms how global executives access and apply academic research on digital transformation.

Client state

Research-rich institute with dense academic output and a growing executive membership seeking actionable insights on AI and digital transformation.

Core challenge

Content was dense, delivery was passive, personalization was nonexistent — executives couldn’t find insights specific to their industry or role.

What we built

Research framework, executive archetypes, AI design principles, information architecture, and a proposed AI-powered research assistant.

Direction

A personalized, AI-driven platform grounded in behavioral archetypes — from passive content delivery to adaptive executive intelligence.

01 · Project context

Bridging the gap between academic rigor and executive action.

Harvard's Digital, Data, and Design (D³) Institute produces and shares research-rooted knowledge on how society and business interact with artificial intelligence and digital technologies.

D³ serves a global membership of C-suite leaders, CTOs, VPs, and senior professionals across industries — from banking and consulting to hospitality and development. Its conferences, curated insights, and research papers represent some of the most rigorous thinking on AI-driven transformation.

Yet the Institute’s digital presence had not kept pace with its audience’s needs. Members relied on fragmented channels — email newsletters, Slack updates, and full-length academic papers — to access content that was often too dense to act on and too broad to feel personal.

“Even 2 or 3 more sentences to give that context will be really valuable.”
— Feedback from D³ member interview

03 · The proposal

Two strategic bets for the D³ platform.

Intelligaia proposed two foundational design strategies — each informed by research, each requiring the AI assistant to behave differently for different users.

01
STRATEGY

Archetype-based personalization.

Use executive decision-making archetypes — derived from literature study — to tailor content delivery, recommendations, and AI interactions to each user’s strategic profile rather than surface-level preferences.

02
STRATEGY

AI principles as reasoned tradeoffs.

Frame every AI design principle as a genuine tradeoff where both sides are plausible — forcing the team to confront real choices like Conclusion-First vs. Reasoning-First rather than defaulting to generic truisms.

04 · Research approach

Multi-method research across three evidence layers.

Rather than relying on a single research stream, Intelligaia layered qualitative interviews, quantitative survey analysis, and academic literature synthesis to build a design-ready framework.

Method What it revealed Scale
Qualitative interviews How executives consume, evaluate, and apply research. Their preferred formats, sources, and frustrations with existing content delivery. 5+ in-depth sessions with executives across development, consulting, banking, and hospitality
Survey analysis Behavioral archetypes among conference attendees. Four distinct engagement patterns emerged from decomposing open-ended feedback into themes. 600+ attendees of D³’s ‘Leading with AI’ conference
Literature study Academic frameworks for AI-driven executive decision-making. Strategic tensions that differentiate how leaders approach AI adoption. 5 research volumes synthesized into a design-ready archetype framework

Three research layers — what each contributed

QUALITATIVE

Executive voice

In-depth interviews revealed that executives wanted deeper engagement — connections to active research, networking with peers solving similar problems, and content specific to their industry context.

BEHAVIORAL

06 · AI design principles

Principles framed as reasoned tradeoffs, not truisms.

Rather than defaulting to generic AI principles — ‘be trustworthy,’ ‘prioritize the user’ — Intelligaia developed each principle as a genuine tradeoff where both sides are plausible.

This forced the team to confront real design choices. The central tradeoff — Conclusion-First vs. Reasoning-First — determined whether the AI assistant would lead with strategic takeaways or walk users through the data to derive their own conclusions.

TRADEOFF 01

Conclusion-first vs. Reasoning-first

Should the AI lead with the strategic takeaway, or guide users through the evidence? The answer depends on the archetype — time-constrained executives prefer conclusions; analytical leaders prefer reasoning.

TRADEOFF 02

Breadth vs. Depth

Should the platform surface a wide range of topics or go deep on a few? Personalization by archetype resolved this — each user sees depth in their domain and breadth as discovery.

TRADEOFF 03

Curation vs. Exploration

Should AI curate a focused feed or enable open exploration? The design balanced both — a personalized dashboard for daily use and a search interface for deeper research moments.

Each principle was tested against the four executive archetypes. A principle that worked for Legacy Navigators but failed for Urgent Executioners was refined until it held across all profiles.

09 · Executive archetypes

Four archetypes that shaped the AI’s behavior.

The literature study produced four executive decision-making archetypes — each defined by the strategic tensions they navigate and the type of AI support they need.

ARCHETYPE 01

Legacy Navigators

Leaders in slow-moving, durable systems navigating inherited complexity. They value deep context, historical grounding, and AI that surfaces institutional knowledge before recommending change.

ARCHETYPE 02

Urgent Executioners

Operating under tight structural constraints with no margin for improvisation. They need AI that delivers conclusions first, prioritizes speed, and flags only the decisions that require human judgment.

ARCHETYPE 03

Coordinated Builders

Thriving in modular systems where value depends on precise cross-functional timing. They prefer AI that maps dependencies, sequences actions, and highlights coordination risks across teams.

ARCHETYPE 04

Principled Orchestrators

Navigating long-horizon decisions where ethical tension and systemic integration dominate. They need AI that surfaces tradeoffs transparently and supports reasoning-first exploration.
