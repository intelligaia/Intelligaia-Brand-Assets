# Usability Evidence & Prioritization

## 1. Evidence Sources
Potential sources include:
user interviews; contextual inquiry; usability testing; screen recordings; support cases; workflow observation; analytics; error logs; search behavior; abandonment; training materials; survey feedback; stakeholder interviews; implementation artifacts.

Each source answers different questions.

## 2. Evidence Record
For each finding capture:
source → date/context → role → task → observed/quoted evidence → frequency evidence → consequence evidence → confidence → limitations.

## 3. Observation vs Interpretation
Separate:
**Observed:** what the user/system actually did.
**Interpretation:** why it may have happened.
**Proposal:** what might improve it.

Do not collapse these into one statement.

## 4. Frequency
Use measured frequency where available.

If frequency is not known, write **Unknown / Validation Needed** rather than “common.”

## 5. Severity
Severity should reflect consequences such as:
task blocked; incorrect commercial outcome; customer impact; rework; delay; support dependency; data-integrity risk; financial risk; cognitive burden.

Do not assign numeric severity without an agreed scale/evidence.

## 6. Reach
Determine which roles, transaction types, products, regions, channels, lifecycle stages, or volumes are affected.

## 7. Confidence
Possible qualitative labels:
high confidence; moderate confidence; low confidence/hypothesis.

Define project-specific criteria before using quantitative scores.

## 8. Business Impact
Potential dimensions:
cycle time; conversion; seller capacity; approval delay; order fallout; support effort; billing correction; renewal risk; partner effort.

Impact must be evidenced or framed as a hypothesis.

## 9. User Impact
Potential dimensions:
time; effort; comprehension; error risk; interruption; inability to complete; loss of trust.

## 10. Risk
Give special attention to UX that can cause:
wrong customer; wrong product/configuration; wrong price/discount; wrong version; duplicate order; incorrect effective date; unintended cancellation; billing/service mismatch.

## 11. Prioritization
A useful decision frame:
**Evidence Strength × User/Business Consequence × Reach × Strategic Relevance × Feasibility/Dependency**

This is a framework, not a mandated numeric formula.

## 12. Quick Win
Do not label something a quick win before validating technical/process dependencies.

## 13. Strategic Opportunity
A high-impact issue may require catalog, pricing, integration, policy or operating-model change rather than a UI-only fix.

## 14. Contradictory Evidence
Preserve disagreement:
role A reports problem → role B does not → analytics uncertain → research needed.

Do not force consensus.

## 15. Historical Findings
Mark older research as **Historical** and validate against current workflow before prioritizing.

## 16. Sample Bias
Record who was studied and who was not. A few expert users may not represent partners, new sellers, operations, regions, or high-volume teams.

## 17. Success Metric
For each prioritized issue define what evidence would demonstrate improvement.

Examples of metric types:
completion; error; rework; elapsed time; support contacts; abandonment; comprehension; successful recovery.

Do not invent baselines/targets.

## 18. Prioritization Output
Recommended fields:
ID → finding → role/journey → evidence → consequence → reach → confidence → dependencies → priority rationale → opportunity → metric needed.

## Guardrail
Never fabricate quantitative evidence to make prioritization appear more rigorous.
