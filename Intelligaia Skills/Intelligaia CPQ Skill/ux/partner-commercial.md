# Partner Experience — Commercial, Deal & Approval UX

## 1. Commercial Visibility
Define role-based visibility for:
reference/list price → partner price → customer price → requested discount → approved discount → margin-sensitive/internal values.

Not all values should be visible to every role.

## 2. Price Meaning
Labels should make the business meaning of visible prices explicit. Avoid ambiguous “price” when several commercial bases exist.

## 3. Discount Request
Where partners can request exceptions:
requested commercial change → rationale → supporting deal context → approval consequence → resulting approved value.

## 4. Deal Registration / Special Pricing
If applicable, preserve correlation:
partner → customer → deal/program request → validity → quote/version → approved commercial treatment.

Exact program names/rules require evidence.

## 5. Eligibility Feedback
When a commercial program or discount is unavailable, explain the business-level reason where permitted and the next action.

## 6. Confidentiality
Do not expose internal cost, margin, approval commentary, other partners' data or internal-only commercial rules unless explicitly authorized.

## 7. Approval Context
Partner-facing approval state should communicate:
submitted → pending → action required → approved → rejected/expired/superseded as supported.

## 8. Rejection
Return actionable reason at the permitted level without leaking confidential internal decision logic.

## 9. Validity
Make quote, price, program or approval validity dates visible where they affect partner action.

## 10. Version Integrity
Special pricing/approval must remain tied to the intended quote/version and material revisions should trigger the governed revalidation path.

## 11. Customer Price
Where partner sets customer-facing price, clearly distinguish it from vendor-to-partner commercial values.

## 12. Distributor Commercials
If distribution is involved, do not collapse vendor→distributor, distributor→partner and partner→customer commercial layers into one ambiguous value.

## 13. Currency
Show applicable currency consistently and explain restrictions where supported.

## 14. Commercial Comparison
For revisions show decision-relevant changes without exposing unauthorized internal values.

## 15. Expiry
Before an expiring approval/program makes a quote unusable, show the relevant business warning where feasible.

## Evaluation Questions
- Can the partner tell which price they are viewing?
- Are confidential values protected?
- Is special pricing tied to the right quote/version?
- Can the partner understand why approval is pending?
- Are validity/expiry consequences visible?
- Are channel commercial layers distinguishable?

## Guardrail
Do not invent discounts, margins, program rules, partner pricing formulas, approval thresholds or data-access policies.
