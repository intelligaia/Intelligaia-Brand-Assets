Foundations · Principles

How design decides.

Three principles govern every visual and functional call. When two are in tension, resolve toward the one earlier in the list.

Philosophy

Design is a tool, not a taste. It exists to make the person on the other side more capable — never to impress the person making it.

— Intelligaia design philosophy

Principle 01
Goodness leads

Serve the person, not the maker.

Every design decision passes a single test: does it make the person on the other side more capable, more informed, or more at ease? If the answer requires justification about brand, ego, or novelty — remove it and try again.

Do

  • Choose clarity over cleverness in every headline
  • Show progress and current state in every flow
  • Let content set the layout, not the reverse

Don't

  • Add motion, illustration or texture that doesn't serve comprehension
  • Hide primary actions behind aesthetic choices
  • Optimize for portfolio screenshots
Principle 02
Intent-aware

Every pixel earns its place.

Interfaces are the sum of user intents. Every element visible on screen must serve the intent the user has right now — not one they might have later. Postpone. Remove. When in doubt, cut.

Do

  • Surface the next best action given current context
  • Use progressive disclosure for advanced capabilities
  • Reveal density on demand — not by default

Don't

  • Populate dashboards with every metric that could exist
  • Show empty states as blank space — treat them as first-run flows
  • Add tabs to postpone hard content decisions
Principle 03
Human-centered

Machines assist. Humans decide.

Agents write, generate, propose. Humans review, edit, ship. Interfaces stay legible and reversible. Automated actions are labelled as such. Consent is explicit — not buried.

Do

  • Label AI-generated content clearly and consistently
  • Provide a way to undo any automated action
  • Show the reasoning — sources, confidence, provenance

Don't

  • Auto-apply agent-suggested changes without human review
  • Hide the "agent did this" attribution to feel more magical
  • Present opaque confidence as certainty
