Palette.

Three ways to reduce color to something disciplined and intentional. The palette decides the temperature of an illustration before a single shape is drawn — choose it first, defend it later.

A1.1 · A

Anchored.

Definition One hue with a four-step ladder, ink, and surface. Calm, focused, scales easily across an entire set. The default for single-subject heroes and agent personas.
Recommended subject — a single device (laptop or workstation) rendered in one hue ladder. Same object across A / B / C — only the palette logic changes.

Rules — Anchored
  • The 4-step ladder must keep ≥ 4.5:1 contrast between the lightest and darkest shade.
  • Background tone is part of the palette — choose surface before you start drawing.
  • No second hue. If you need one, you've moved into Layered.
  • Recommended use — single-subject heroes, agent personas, single-icon spots.
B1.1 · B

Layered.

Definition Base hue ladder + one accent + a neutral. The everyday working palette — disciplined, with room to point. The accent is where the brand yellow lives.
Recommended subject — same device + one accent prop (coffee mug, plant, sticker). Yellow accent must never exceed 10% of total area.

Rules — Layered
  • Accent area should never exceed 10% of the total image — it directs attention, not dominates it.
  • Yellow is the locked Intelligaia accent. Other hues (cyan, magenta from the gradient set) wait until tokens are confirmed.
  • Neutral carries the composition. Keep it warm (cream) rather than cold (true gray).
  • Recommended use — most editorial & spot illustrations, blog headers, doc illustrations.
C1.1 · C

Spectrum.

Definition Three balanced hues plus a neutral background. The story-mode palette — used when an illustration must carry a narrative on its own.
Recommended subject — full workspace scene (desk + monitor + chair + plants). Balance hues with the 60 / 30 / 10 area rule.

Rules — Spectrum
  • 60 / 30 / 10 area rule — Anchor dominates, Counter supports, Neutral grounds.
  • Always have a background hue. White is allowed but ranks as "no background."
  • Reserved for hero, cover and narrative campaign work. Not for inline or UI illustrations.
  • Use this palette sparingly — overuse dilutes the framework.
