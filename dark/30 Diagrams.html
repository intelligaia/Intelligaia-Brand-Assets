Diagrams

Systems, made legible.

Process flows, matrices, hub maps and journeys — the four patterns Intelligaia uses to explain how something works.

Purpose

Explain a system without a paragraph.

Diagrams carry process, hierarchy and relationship. They use the same geometry and palette as the illustration library, but obey stricter rules — legibility beats expression every time.

Geometry

Circle · square · diamond

Node shape encodes type. Never decorative.

Flow

Left to right

Or top to bottom. Never both in one diagram.

Colour

One accent

Yellow for the active path. Grey for everything else.

Labels

Three words max

If a node needs a sentence, the diagram is wrong.

Types

Four diagram patterns.

Pick the pattern that matches the relationship you're showing. Don't invent a fifth.

Process flow

Linear sequence. Steps connected left to right with dashed links.

Two-by-two matrix

Positioning on two axes. Impact vs effort, reach vs depth.

Hub and spoke

One centre, many dependents. Ecosystem and platform maps.

Journey / timeline

Phases over time. Emotional curve or stage progression.
Rules

Do & don't.

Do

  • One accent colour per diagram — yellow marks the path that matters
  • Keep node labels to three words or fewer
  • Use dashed lines for flow, solid for structure
  • Leave generous whitespace between nodes

Don't

  • No more than seven nodes in a single view
  • No crossing connector lines — restructure instead
  • No drop shadows, bevels or 3D perspective
  • No colour used decoratively — every hue means something
